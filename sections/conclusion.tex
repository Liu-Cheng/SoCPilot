\section{Conclusion}
This paper presents SoCPilot, an agentic framework for application-driven CPU--accelerator SoC customization. SoCPilot coordinates requirement interpretation, application profiling, hardware/software partitioning, accelerator generation, SoC configuration and integration, and latency--area design space exploration. FPGA execution validates the generated designs through complete-application correctness and latency measurements.

Across four FPGA-validated applications, SoCPilot achieves a geometric mean end-to-end speedup of 2.66$\times$ over matched CPU-only implementations. In the recorded runs, a DeepSeek-based general-purpose agent following the HSCO-Bench protocol fails to complete the evaluated pre-FPGA workflow on all five applications. These results support the usefulness of a structured, tool-guided workflow for SoC customization, while differences in the configured toolchains limit causal attribution. Evaluation across multiple area budgets and controlled comparisons of partitioning and IP selection remain to be completed. Future work will broaden system-level cost models and validation coverage.
